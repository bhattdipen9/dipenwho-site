      How the score works
      1. Clean. For 10-K/10-Qs the MD&A section is extracted. For transcripts, speakers are detected, analysts and the operator are removed, and prepared remarks are separated from Q&A. Safe-harbor and call-logistics sentences, page numbers and table rows are dropped.
      2. ER phrases first. Multi-word expressions like free cash flow positive (+3), covenant waiver (−3) or going concern (−5) are matched before single words, so they aren't double counted.
      3. Metric × move. A metric paired with a direction word within five words: gross margin … compressed, raising … guidance, churn … increased. Each metric knows whether "up" is good (revenue) or bad (churn, leverage, DSO).
      4. Single words from a finance-aware list (or the Loughran-McDonald dictionary, if loaded).
      5. Modifiers. Negation (not yet FCF positive) flips and halves a score; intensifiers (significantly) scale it up and diminishers (slightly) scale it down; hedged sentences (may, could, potentially) count 60%.
      6. Score = (positive − negative) ÷ (positive + negative) × 100. Forward-looking and backward-looking sentences are also scored separately.
      7. Comparison. Change in score, a bootstrap confidence interval (sentences resampled 1,000×) to tell real shifts from noise, language similarity, and the phrases that appeared, disappeared or changed in frequency.
      Optional: load the Loughran-McDonald dictionary. Using built-in starter word list.
      For research and educational purposes only. Not investment advice. Scores are dictionary-based and can misread context.
