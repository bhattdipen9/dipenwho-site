## Executive Summary

nVent has transformed from a diversified electrical-products company into a more focused electrical connection and systems-protection platform. The strongest evidence is company-specific: Infrastructure represented 45% of 2025 sales, company-wide data-center sales were approximately $1.0 billion, Systems Protection generated 72% of Q1 2026 revenue, and Q1 Systems Protection organic sales grew 50.1%.[1][2][3]

The operating thesis is constructive. AI data centers require denser power distribution, cable management, system protection, engineered buildings, and increasingly liquid cooling. These are physical bottlenecks that nVent addresses directly. Power-utility investment and broader electrification provide a second demand leg beyond data centers.

The valuation conclusion is more restrained. Our base case produces a $182.25 target, or 11.9% upside from the June 5 close. That remains below the model's 15% Buy threshold, while the DCF cross-check is materially lower at approximately $98.96 per share. We therefore rate nVent Neutral / Market Perform: a strong company and credible infrastructure compounder, but one whose premium valuation still requires sustained execution.

## Investment Thesis

### 1. Data centers are already a core business, not a distant option

nVent's 2026 Investor Day showed approximately $1.0 billion of 2025 data-center sales and an approximately 40% data-center sales CAGR from 2022 through 2025.[3] This scale matters: data centers are large enough to affect consolidated growth rather than merely provide narrative optionality.

The product fit is direct. nVent supplies enclosures, liquid and air cooling, power management, engineered buildings, switchgear and bus systems, cable pathways, grounding and bonding, power connections, and equipment protection. These products help connect, protect, cool, and manage mission-critical electrical infrastructure.

### 2. Systems Protection is the economic engine of the thesis

Systems Protection generated $894.8 million of Q1 2026 revenue, representing 72.0% of the company total. Reported growth was 76.1%, including 50.1% organic growth, and segment return on sales was 22.7%.[2]

Electrical Connections remains important, but its role is different. It generated $347.2 million of Q1 revenue, with 15.3% reported and 7.9% organic growth. It provides a profitable base tied to utility, industrial, construction, and electrical installation demand, while Systems Protection carries the more direct data-center, cooling, and engineered-infrastructure exposure.

### 3. Portfolio transformation has increased infrastructure exposure

nVent has deliberately changed its business mix:

- It acquired ECM Industries in 2023 for approximately $1.1 billion, expanding electrical connectors, tools, test instruments, and cable management.
- It acquired Trachte in 2024 for approximately $0.7 billion, adding engineered control buildings for critical infrastructure.
- It completed the sale of Thermal Management on January 30, 2025, receiving approximately $1.6 billion of net cash proceeds.
- It acquired the Electrical Products Group on May 1, 2025 for approximately $1.0 billion, adding enclosures, switchgear, and bus systems serving power utilities and data centers.

The result is a more focused electrical platform. At 2025 Investor Day, Infrastructure represented 45% of sales and Systems Protection represented 67%.

### 4. Electrification and power utilities provide a second growth leg

The thesis does not depend solely on hyperscaler capital spending. Data-center construction creates demand inside the facility, while the associated electrical load creates demand for utility interconnections, substations, grid upgrades, control buildings, switchgear, and power connections.

This is derived demand: investment by data-center operators and utilities creates demand for nVent's enabling products. It also creates bottleneck economics. When power, cooling, and interconnection capacity constrain deployment, customers must spend on the infrastructure that relieves those constraints.

### 5. Orders, backlog, and guidance indicate near-term momentum

In Q1 2026, nVent reported approximately 40% organic orders growth and a record $2.6 billion backlog. Management raised full-year guidance from 15%-18% reported sales growth and 10%-13% organic growth to 26%-28% reported and 21%-23% organic growth. Updated adjusted EPS guidance was $4.45-$4.55.[2]

These indicators do not guarantee future growth, but they show that the thesis is already visible in orders, sales, backlog, and management expectations.

## Industry Backdrop

The AI data-center cycle is increasingly a power and infrastructure cycle. The International Energy Agency reported that data-center electricity demand increased 17% in 2025, while capital expenditure by five large technology companies exceeded $400 billion and was expected to rise a further 75% in 2026.[4]

U.S. grid implications are also significant. The Department of Energy, citing Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, estimates that data centers consumed approximately 4.4% of U.S. electricity in 2023 and could consume 6.7%-12.0% by 2028.[5]

AI raises the technical burden within the data center. Uptime Institute reports that current-generation AI systems can exceed 40 kW per rack and some 2025-generation deployments can exceed 100 kW. It describes liquid cooling as typically used above 50 kW per rack or for specialized high-performance equipment.[6]

The economic implication is straightforward: higher rack density increases the value of reliable power distribution, thermal management, protection, modular infrastructure, and serviceability. This supports nVent's addressable opportunity, but customer qualification cycles, project timing, permitting, and grid constraints can make demand lumpy.

## Historical Financial Analysis

### 2025 growth was strong, but acquisitions were a major contributor

Net sales rose 29.5% to $3,893.1 million in 2025. Organic growth contributed 12.6 percentage points, acquisitions contributed 16.3 points, and currency contributed 0.6 points.

Systems Protection sales increased 42.2%, including 17.1% organic growth and 24.3% acquisition growth. Electrical Connections increased 9.9%, including 5.7% organic and 3.9% acquisition growth. The acquisition contribution must be separated from organic demand when evaluating quality of growth.

### Margins reveal the cost of rapid expansion

Gross margin declined from 40.2% in 2024 to 37.7% in 2025, while consolidated operating margin declined from 17.5% to 15.8%. Systems Protection segment margin declined 140 basis points to 20.7%; Electrical Connections declined 130 basis points to 28.7%.

Management attributed pressure to inflation, tariffs, labor, raw materials, and investments in capacity and growth. SG&A as a percentage of sales improved by 60 basis points, demonstrating fixed-cost leverage, but it was not sufficient to offset gross-margin pressure and higher amortization.

### Cash flow and leverage improved

Free cash flow from continuing operations increased to $561.0 million from $427.5 million in 2024. Free cash flow conversion was 131% of continuing net income and free cash flow margin was 14.4%.

Total debt declined to $1,559.8 million from $2,155.0 million, and net debt declined to approximately $1,322.3 million from $2,023.8 million. The Thermal Management sale was non-recurring, but it materially improved balance-sheet flexibility.

### Revenue assumptions

Systems Protection grows 36% in 2026, then 15%, 10%, and 8% from 2027 through 2029. Electrical Connections grows 9% in 2026, followed by 6%, 5%, and 4%.

The deceleration does not contradict the thesis. Economic mean reversion and the law of large numbers make constant 30%-50% growth implausible. The thesis remains supported if post-2026 growth stays above normal industrial and electrical end-market growth while the segment mix shifts toward Systems Protection.

### Margin assumptions

Operating margin rises from 15.8% in 2025 to 19.0% by 2029. The forecast assumes operating leverage, productivity, and a better mix, but does not assume that tariffs, labor, capacity spending, and raw-material pressure disappear.

### Cash-flow assumptions

Free cash flow rises to approximately $1.05 billion by 2029. Working capital is already captured within operating cash flow; it is not subtracted a second time in the free-cash-flow calculation.

### DCF cross-check

The base DCF implies approximately $98.96 per share using a 9.0% WACC and 4.0% terminal growth rate. The large gap versus the market price indicates that investors are assigning value to growth and premium economics beyond the explicit forecast. The DCF therefore serves as a valuation-risk warning rather than the primary target.

### Peer framework

nVent sits between two peer groups:

- Higher-growth data-center and power-infrastructure references: Vertiv, Eaton, and Schneider Electric.
- Traditional electrical and utility-infrastructure anchors: Legrand and Hubbell.

Vertiv is an upside reference, not a direct base-case comp, because it has purer exposure to critical digital infrastructure. Eaton and Schneider are relevant to power management and data centers but are much larger and more diversified. Legrand and Hubbell provide more conservative electrical-infrastructure anchors.

The re-rating thesis depends on nVent sustaining growth and margins closer to the first group than the second. The refreshed June 8, 2026 peer screen shows nVent at 33.73x forward P/E and 30.13x EV/EBITDA, above traditional electrical anchors but below Vertiv's 44.03x forward P/E and 48.76x EV/EBITDA.[7]

## Catalysts

- Continued organic orders and backlog growth in data centers and power utilities.
- Systems Protection organic growth remaining above company growth after acquisition anniversaries.
- Faster adoption of liquid cooling and higher content per data-center megawatt.
- Margin recovery as pricing and productivity offset tariffs and inflation.
- Upward revisions to 2027-2029 earnings estimates.
- Successful integration and cross-selling of Electrical Products Group and Trachte capabilities.

## Risks

- Data-center project delays, permitting constraints, power shortages, or customer capital-spending cuts.
- Growth proving to be pull-forward or backlog conversion rather than durable demand.
- Acquisition integration risk and lower-than-expected synergies.
- Tariffs, copper, steel, electronics, freight, and labor inflation.
- Systems Protection margin failing to recover despite high growth.
- Competition from Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider Electric, Legrand, Hubbell, ABB, and specialized cooling or electrical suppliers.
- Technology risk if liquid-cooling architectures evolve away from nVent's strongest offerings.
- Multiple compression if investors rotate away from premium AI-infrastructure equities.

## What to Monitor

The thesis should be reassessed each quarter using seven indicators:

1. Systems Protection organic sales growth.
2. Data-center sales, orders, and backlog commentary.
3. Systems Protection segment margin.
4. Organic growth versus acquisition contribution.
5. Free cash flow conversion and working-capital use.
6. Net debt and acquisition-related capital allocation.
7. Forward valuation relative to Eaton, Schneider, Vertiv, Legrand, and Hubbell.

## Conclusion

nVent has credible, measurable exposure to AI data-center and electrical-infrastructure spending. The company has deliberately transformed its portfolio, Systems Protection is producing exceptional organic growth, and data centers are already a material revenue category.

The investment debate is therefore not whether nVent benefits from AI infrastructure. It does. The debate is whether that growth remains durable enough to justify a premium valuation after 2026. Our base case supports continued earnings and cash-flow growth, but the current risk/reward remains below the model's Buy threshold. We recommend Neutral / Market Perform with a $182.25 target.
